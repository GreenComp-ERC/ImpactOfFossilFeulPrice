\section{Introduction}

The urgency of mitigating global warming necessitates a rapid energy transition to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) \cite{messerli2019global}. Since energy consumption is the primary source of global greenhouse gas emissions \cite{BOGDANOV2021120467}, a transformation of energy systems is essential. This transition is not solely a technological endeavor \cite{CHEN20191564}; it also encompasses critical economic dimensions, among which the weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is a critical metric \cite{BACHNER2019100373}. It represents the average return required by capital providers, balancing the costs of debt and equity financing \cite{BACHNER2019100373}. The environmental consequences of this financial indicator are profound; modeling for Africa shows 35\% lower green electricity production under high capital costs in a 2$^\circ$C pathway, while a finance-policy scenario brings net-zero emissions forward by about a decade \cite{ameli2021higher}.

This study aligns with the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), specifically SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy) by informing renewable project finance, SDG 13 (Climate Action) by examining financing conditions relevant to decarbonization, SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure) through an open, reproducible three-stage machine learning pipeline, and SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals) by releasing harmonized multi-regional panels to support global research collaboration.

A pernicious challenge for the energy transition is its inherent vulnerability to shocks from the legacy fossil fuel system it seeks to replace. Volatility in global oil, gas, and coal prices propagates through the financial system, influencing inflation, interest rates, and investor risk perception, potentially affecting the WACC for capital-intensive renewable projects \cite{melodia2022energy,dobrowolski2022weighted}. This financial vulnerability can increase environmental risk, as high capital costs can slow the pace of decarbonization \cite{egli2019bias,ameli2021higher}. Existing research has examined WACC and interest rates under unstable market conditions \cite{dobrowolski2022weighted}. However, less attention has been devoted to evaluating its influence on WACC within the context of energy transition projects. Additionally, the varied impacts of fossil fuel price volatility across different energy sectors and regions have not been fully explored.

Therefore, this study focuses on the upcoming decade and seeks to answer the following questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How do fossil fuel price fluctuations affect the WACC of energy transition projects, influencing the pace of renewable energy deployment?
    \item How do these price dynamics relate to capital costs across regions, particularly between fuel-importing and fuel-exporting countries, and how should financing strategies for low-carbon projects be shaped?
\end{itemize}

This work contributes an open and reproducible AI framework that translates fossil fuel price signals into actionable climate finance intelligence. By integrating a region--fuel--project tensor with an AutoGluon-based forecasting pipeline, the framework provides decision support for analyzing regional differences in financing conditions, energy costs, and decarbonization pathways. Its outputs can inform efforts to reduce household energy burdens, improve environmental quality, and support more effective climate and energy policy across diverse global regions.
