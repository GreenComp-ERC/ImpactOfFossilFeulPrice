\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Regional Differences}

\subsubsection{Europe}
The original European specifications have the strongest average in-sample fit ($R^2=0.670$). Europe's reliance on imported energy provides a relevant context for examining international price exposure. Rom\'an-Collado and Casado Ru\'iz identify energy dependence, economic activity, and efficiency as drivers of EU-27 energy imports over 2000--2020 \cite{ROMANCOLLADO2024108009}. Li and Leung examine links between energy prices, economic growth, and renewable-energy consumption in Europe \cite{LI20211712}.

\subsubsection{Structural Dependence and Policy Limitations}
Carbon pricing and renewable-energy policy shape the investment environment. The EU ETS literature emphasizes the importance of policy design and innovation incentives \cite{EUETS}, while the Renewable Energy Directive provides a framework for renewable deployment \cite{RenewableEnergyDirective}. Policy credibility and predictability matter for both investment risk and expected returns \cite{POLZIN20191249}. These mechanisms motivate explicit policy scenarios in future financing analysis.

\subsubsection{External Shocks and Financial Risks}
The European energy crisis illustrates the importance of energy security and storage planning \cite{WEI2024110522}. Fiscal instruments can also alter the pass-through of energy-price shocks to inflation and output in energy-importing economies \cite{GLOCKER2024103201}. Financing instruments provide a complementary channel: green-bond certification and issuance can influence investor participation and the credibility of environmental commitments \cite{ehlers2017green,flammer2021corporate}.

\subsubsection{China}
China's original average fit is $R^2=0.650$. Research finds time-varying spillovers between international and Chinese energy and commodity markets \cite{SUN2022121642,WANG2023128724}. These links provide a rationale for including international fuel-price signals in regional financing analysis.

\subsubsection{Import Dependence}
Ben-Salha et al. find long-run relationships between fossil fuel prices and renewable-energy consumption in China, but no significant short-run effects in their specification \cite{ben2022assessing}. Their results distinguish short-run price exposure from longer-run substitution toward renewables.

\subsubsection{Government Intervention}
Price regulation can modify the domestic consequences of international energy-market changes. He and Lin model the economic and emissions effects of natural-gas price control in China \cite{HE2017524}. Policy design, public finance, and risk mitigation are therefore relevant considerations when interpreting regional financing conditions \cite{POLZIN20191249,duma2023risk}.

\subsubsection{Middle East}
The original Middle Eastern specifications have a lower average fit ($R^2=0.547$). Oil dependence, diversification, and domestic pricing institutions provide possible contextual explanations. Karanfil and Omgba examine structural factors that shape export diversification in oil-dependent economies \cite{karanfil2023energy}.

\subsubsection{Economic Structure}
Fossil fuel revenues may support public investment and financing capacity, while also exposing public budgets to commodity cycles. A fiscal buffer is therefore a hypothesis to examine using sovereign-finance and project-level data. The corrected sensitivity diagnostics show why this distinction matters: lower model fit in the Middle East can coexist with larger estimated price-response coefficients in some technologies (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:appendix-elasticities}).

\subsubsection{Institutional Design}
Fattouh and El-Katiri document the importance and economic trade-offs of energy subsidies in the Middle East and North Africa \cite{FATTOUH2013108}. Such arrangements can affect domestic price exposure and investment incentives. Their effects on renewable-project financing also depend on contract design, counterparty risk, and guarantees \cite{duma2023risk}.

\subsection{Environmental Implications of Financial Sensitivity}
Financing conditions can influence the affordability and timing of renewable investment. Prior modeling shows that regional differences in capital costs can materially change decarbonization pathways \cite{ameli2021higher,calcaterra2024reducing}. The present study adds a framework for comparing fuel-price and inflation signals across regional WACC scenarios. A linked investment or energy-system model could extend the analysis to deployment and emissions.

\subsection{Regional Community Impact}
Reliable energy finance is relevant to affordability and a just transition. European research documents substantial geographical differences in energy poverty \cite{bouzarovski2017energy}, and the OECD emphasizes aligning local climate finance with inclusive development \cite{oecd2021financing}. These concerns motivate analysis of financing conditions in Europe, China, and the Middle East.

\subsection{Climate Finance \& Policy}
In Europe, predictable revenue arrangements and financial instruments such as green bonds, subsidies, and climate risk insurance can support renewable investment by reducing financing risks \cite{POLZIN20191249,duma2023risk,ehlers2017green,flammer2021corporate}.

China's energy transition also involves government intervention in energy markets. Policymakers could consider more market-driven price mechanisms, complemented by targeted green finance initiatives to reduce capital costs. State-supported financing can help encourage private investment in clean energy while preserving broader economic stability \cite{HE2017524,POLZIN20191249,duma2023risk}.

For the Middle East, dependence on fossil fuel revenues makes diversification important for long-term economic resilience. Policymakers could prioritize investment in renewable energy, with sovereign financing capacity providing a possible source of long-term capital \cite{karanfil2023energy,FATTOUH2013108}.
